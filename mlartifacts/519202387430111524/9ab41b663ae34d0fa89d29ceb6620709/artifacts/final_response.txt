Short answer: Generally you must notify your credit card issuer within 60 days after you receive the first monthly statement that shows the airline charge.

How to proceed (recommended)
- Send your dispute in writing to the address listed on your monthly statement for disputing charges (this may be different from the payment address).  
- Include: your account number; a copy of the ticket/itinerary/receipt; if you used part of the ticket, identify used vs. unused segments; and state that the airline is in bankruptcy, you did not receive the service, and you are requesting a credit under the Fair Credit Billing Act.  
- File the dispute as soon as possible — the 60‑day rule is a general deadline and card issuers may have additional procedures.

Notes and extra guidance
- Some card rules or state laws may vary, so if you’re near the 60‑day mark contact your card issuer immediately and keep copies of everything.  
- If you paid with a debit card or have travel insurance, different rules apply — contact your bank and insurer for their timelines and claim procedures.  
- You can also consider filing a proof of claim in the airline’s bankruptcy, but recovery there can be limited and slow.
