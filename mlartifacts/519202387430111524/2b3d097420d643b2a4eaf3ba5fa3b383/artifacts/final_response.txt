The excerpt in our knowledge base does not list any specific, mandatory technical features that a travel voucher must have (for example: exact wording, required fields, or a required expiration length). What the rule does say is:

- Airlines may offer travel credits or vouchers instead of refunds when a passenger cannot travel because of a serious communicable disease (government travel restriction, or medical advice/not to travel).  
- Airlines are permitted to require documentation (medical note or government directive) to support the passenger’s request for a credit/voucher.  
- For ticket‑agent‑booked itineraries, the carrier may rely on partners or agents to help process and issue the voucher and to supply necessary information (contact details, price paid, etc.).

Because the rule text you provided does not define mandatory voucher characteristics, here is practical guidance on what to ask for and confirm when you are issued a voucher so you know your rights and options:

Ask the airline (or the ticket agent) to put the following in writing:
- Voucher issuer (airline name) and contact information.  
- Passenger name and original booking reference(s).  
- Voucher value or amount (face value and any remaining balance).  
- What bookings or fees the voucher may be applied to (fare classes, taxes, fees).  
- Expiration date and any extension/renewal policy.  
- Transferability (whether usable only by the original passenger or transferable).  
- Any blackout dates, fare restrictions, or additional fees for use.  
- Whether codeshare/partner airlines will accept the voucher.  
- Redemption process (how to apply it when rebooking, including any promo codes).  
- Whether it can be combined with other discounts or promotions.  
- Policy if the airline later cancels or changes the new booking made with the voucher.  
- Any documentation the airline required to issue the voucher (keep copies).

Also:
- If you are claiming inability to travel because of a government restriction or quarantine, be prepared to provide the government order or official guidance showing you were prohibited or required to quarantine for a substantial portion of the trip.  
- If you are claiming medical advice, get current documentation from your treating/licensed medical professional stating you were advised not to travel. Airlines may require this.  
- Keep all emails, receipts, and a copy/photo of the voucher and the supporting documentation.
