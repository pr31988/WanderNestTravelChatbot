Generally you must notify your credit card issuer within 60 days. The Fair Credit Billing Act requires that the card issuer receive your written dispute no later than 60 days after the date you received the first monthly statement that showed the charge for the transportation.

Practical steps (follow these exactly):
- Write to the dispute address shown on your monthly statement (this can be different from the payment address).  
- Include your account number, a photocopy of the ticket/itinerary/receipt, and if any travel was used, identify the used and unused segments.  
- State that the airline is in bankruptcy/has ceased operations, you did not receive the service, and you are requesting a credit pursuant to the Fair Credit Billing Act.  
- File as soon as possible — don’t wait until the 60th day.  

Note: some issuers may have additional procedures or shorter time limits, and debit cards and travel insurance follow different rules. If you also want recovery from the airline’s bankruptcy estate, you can file a proof of claim in the bankruptcy, but recoveries there are often small and slow.
