Short answer: Sometimes. A ticket agent may keep a service charge related to issuing the original ticket even if the airline cancelled or significantly changed the flight and the passenger is due a refund — but only if certain conditions are met.

Key points from DOT guidance:
- Ticket agents may retain a service/issuance charge to the extent that the fee is for services beyond merely processing payment for a flight the consumer found (i.e., a ticket‑issuance/service fee), because that service was already provided regardless of travel.  
- That fee must be charged on a per‑passenger basis and its existence, amount, and non‑refundable nature (if applicable) must be clearly and prominently disclosed at the time of purchase.  
- If the fee was not clearly and prominently disclosed, the Department considers retaining it a deceptive practice and would not allow it.  
- The Department did not resolve whether a pure booking/payment‑processing fee may be retained by a ticket agent.  
- Refunds must generally be returned in the original form of payment unless the consumer agrees to an equivalent alternative.

What you should do:
- Check your receipt/terms to see whether a per‑passenger service/issuance fee (and its non‑refundable status) was clearly disclosed when you bought the ticket.  
- If it was not disclosed, ask the ticket agent for a full refund and point out DOT guidance; if they still withhold an undisclosed fee, you can file a complaint with the DOT’s Office of Aviation Consumer Protection.  
- If the fee was disclosed, confirm the amount and that it matches what they are retaining.
