Yes. Under DOT guidance, a practice is “deceptive” if it is likely to mislead a consumer, acting reasonably under the circumstances, about a material matter. A “material” matter is one that would likely have affected the consumer’s decision or conduct (for example, the existence or amount of fees, whether a fee is refundable, or other terms affecting the price or privacy of service). The DOT has said undisclosed fees or failing to disclose refundability are examples of deceptive practices and can be investigated and subject to enforcement. 

If you believe an airline or ticket agent misled you about a material matter (including privacy promises or fees), the DOT’s Office of Aviation Consumer Protection can review complaints and take action.
