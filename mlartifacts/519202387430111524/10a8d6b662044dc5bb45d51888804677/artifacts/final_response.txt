Generally you must notify your card issuer within 60 days of the date you received the first monthly statement that listed the airline charge. In other words, the Fair Credit Billing Act dispute notice must be received by the card issuer no later than 60 days after that statement date.

What to do next (per DOT guidance)
- Contact your credit card company as soon as possible and file a dispute.  
- Write to the address on your monthly statement for disputes (this may differ from the payment address).  
- Include: your account number; a copy of the ticket/itinerary/receipt; identification of any used vs. unused flight segments; and a statement that the airline is in bankruptcy, you did not receive the service, and you are requesting credit under the Fair Credit Billing Act.  
- Send your letter by certified mail and keep copies and proof of mailing.

Additional notes
- File the dispute promptly — even if bankruptcy proceedings are ongoing — because missing the 60‑day window can make it harder to get a charge reversed.  
- Bankruptcy proceedings are separate: you can also file a proof of claim in the bankruptcy, but recoveries there may be partial and slow.  
- If you need more specific timing or procedures, check with your card issuer and, if applicable, any travel insurance provider or consumer protection agency.
