Here’s a clear summary of the international baggage rules based on the information we have, plus practical guidance:

What’s not allowed or restricted
- Dangerous goods: Items that could endanger the aircraft or people (as defined by ICAO/IATA Dangerous Goods Regulations and the carrier’s rules) are prohibited. Check the carrier’s website for details.  
- Items prohibited by law: Anything forbidden by the laws of any country you fly from, to, or over cannot be carried.  
- Unsuitable items: The carrier may refuse items because of weight, size or character (for example fragile or perishable goods).  
- Live animals: Generally not allowed in baggage except where the carrier’s specific live-animal rules apply (see the carrier’s live animal policy).  
- Firearms & ammunition: Firearms/ammunition other than for hunting/sporting purposes are prohibited. Firearms for hunting/sporting may be accepted as checked baggage only under strict conditions: they must be unloaded, have the safety catch on, and be suitably packed; carriage of ammunition is subject to ICAO/IATA dangerous-goods rules.  
- Don’t put valuables in checked baggage: Passengers should not include money, jewelry, precious metals, negotiable papers, securities, business documents, passports/IDs, or other valuables in checked baggage.

Checked baggage handling and limits
- Tagging & custody: When you check baggage the carrier takes custody and issues a baggage identification tag for each piece.  
- Same aircraft rule: Checked bags will be carried on the same aircraft as the passenger unless that is impracticable; if so, the carrier will send them on the next available flight.  
- Allowances & fees: Free baggage allowance, excess-baggage charges, and rules for declaring excess value vary by carrier and are set out in the carrier’s regulations. Expect to pay for baggage beyond the free allowance and to declare/pay for increased liability/value if needed.

Baggage delays and refunds (international itineraries to/from the U.S.)
- When delay is measured: The delay “clock” for a checked bag starts at the arrival of the flight and ends when the carrier delivers the checked bag.  
- Timeframes for avoiding a refund of baggage fees: For international itineraries involving the U.S., regulators use two standards:  
  - If the non‑stop flight segment to/from the U.S. is 12 hours or less, a 15‑hour minimum standard applies.  
  - If the non‑stop flight segment to/from the U.S. is more than 12 hours, carriers may have up to 30 hours to recover delayed bags before they must refund baggage fees.  
  (Note: the itinerary must include the qualifying international segment to use the 30‑hour standard.)

Practical tips
- Keep passports, travel documents, money, jewelry, electronics and other valuables in your carry‑on.  
- Check the airline’s baggage size, weight limits, fees, and prohibited items on their website well before travel.  
- If you need to transport firearms, live animals or potentially restricted items, contact the carrier in advance to confirm rules and required documentation/packaging.  
- If your checked bag is delayed, report it at the airline’s baggage office immediately at the airport and keep receipts for essentials you must buy; the airline’s delivery and refund obligations depend on the delay measurement described above.
