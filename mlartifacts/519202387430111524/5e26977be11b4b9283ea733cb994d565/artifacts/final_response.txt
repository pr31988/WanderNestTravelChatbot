Yes. Under the DOT’s guidance a practice is “deceptive” when it is “likely to mislead a consumer, acting reasonably under the circumstances, regarding a material matter.” A matter is “material” if it would likely have affected the consumer’s decision or conduct. 

Concrete examples the DOT gives include failing to disclose fees (or that a service fee is non‑refundable) or hiding the existence/amount of a refund fee — omissions that would reasonably lead consumers to the wrong conclusion. Misrepresenting how passenger data will be used or failing to follow an airline’s privacy policy can also be considered deceptive or unfair under DOT rules. 

The DOT can investigate complaints and impose civil penalties when it finds unfair or deceptive practices. If you have a specific situation, gather documentation (communications, receipts, the airline’s privacy policy/terms) and consider filing a complaint with the DOT’s Aviation Consumer Protection office — I can help you prepare that if you’d like.
