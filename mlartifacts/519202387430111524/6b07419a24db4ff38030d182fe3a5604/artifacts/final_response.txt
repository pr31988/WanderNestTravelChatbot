Short answer: Yes — but only in limited circumstances.

What the Department of Transportation says:
- A ticket agent may keep a service charge that was for the ticket-issuing service (i.e., for work beyond simply processing payment) even if the airline later cancels or significantly changes the flight and a refund is due. The Department treats that service as already provided and independent of the ticket’s value.
- Conditions: the fee must be charged on a per-passenger basis and its existence, amount, and any non‑refundable nature must have been clearly and prominently disclosed to the consumer at the time of purchase.
- If a fee (including any refund-processing fee) was not properly disclosed at purchase, retaining it is likely an unfair or deceptive practice and the Department would consider action.

What you can do:
- Check your purchase confirmation/receipt for a clear disclosure of the service fee, its amount, and whether it is non‑refundable.
- Ask the ticket agent for a refund breakdown and written proof that the fee was disclosed at the time of sale.
- Refunds must be provided in the original form of payment unless you agree to another cash-equivalent form.
- If the fee was not disclosed or the agent refuses to refund an undisclosed amount, you can escalate the issue (for example, file a complaint with the U.S. DOT Office of Aviation Consumer Protection).
