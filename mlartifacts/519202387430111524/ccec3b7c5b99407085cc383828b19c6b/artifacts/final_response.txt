Yes. Under DOT guidance a practice is “deceptive” if it is likely to mislead a consumer, acting reasonably under the circumstances, about a material matter. A “material” matter is one that would likely affect the consumer’s decision or conduct (for example, undisclosed fees or refund terms). The DOT gives concrete examples — failing to disclose that a service fee is non‑refundable or not revealing the existence/amount of a refund fee would be considered deceptive. The Department can investigate such practices and impose penalties where appropriate. 

If you believe an airline or ticket agent misled you, keep copies of communications and receipts and you can file a complaint with the DOT’s Aviation Consumer Protection office.
