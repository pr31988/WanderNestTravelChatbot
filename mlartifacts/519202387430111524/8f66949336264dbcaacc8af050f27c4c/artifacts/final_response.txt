Short answer: Yes — but only in limited circumstances.

What the Department of Transportation says
- A ticket agent may keep a service fee that represents the cost of a service already provided (for example, a fee for issuing the ticket) even if the passenger did not travel because the airline cancelled or significantly changed the flight.  
- That retained fee must be:
  - for a service that is more than simply “processing payment” for a flight the consumer found (i.e., not just a basic payment/booking-processing fee),  
  - charged on a per‑passenger basis, and  
  - clearly and prominently disclosed to the consumer at the time of purchase, including the amount and whether the fee is non‑refundable.
- If the agent failed to disclose the fee (or its amount/non‑refundable nature) at purchase, retaining the fee would likely be considered an unfair or deceptive practice and the Department may take action.
- Refunds must be returned in the original form of payment unless you agree to another cash‑equivalent option.

What you should do
- Check your purchase receipt and the agent’s terms for a clear disclosure of the fee, its amount, and whether it is non‑refundable.  
- If the fee was not disclosed, ask the agent for an explanation and request the withheld amount. If you are not satisfied, you can file a complaint with the Department of Transportation’s Office of Aviation Consumer Protection.
