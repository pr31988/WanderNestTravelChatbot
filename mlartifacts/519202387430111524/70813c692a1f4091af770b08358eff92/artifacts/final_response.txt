Yes. Under DOT guidance, a practice is “deceptive” if it is likely to mislead a consumer, acting reasonably under the circumstances, about a material matter. A “material” matter is one that would likely have affected the consumer’s decision or conduct with respect to a product or service. 

That means misleading consumers about privacy practices, fees, refundability, or other important terms can be deemed deceptive. The DOT can investigate such practices and take action (including civil penalties) against airlines or ticket agents that engage in deceptive conduct. 

If you believe an airline or ticket agent misled you about a material matter (for example, failing to disclose a non‑refundable service fee or misrepresenting what personal data will be used or shared), the DOT has authority to investigate complaints and may pursue enforcement. Save any relevant documentation (emails, receipts, screenshots) if you want to report the issue.
