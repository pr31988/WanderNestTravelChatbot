Short answer: Yes — but only in specific circumstances.

What the rule says (plain language)
- A ticket agent may keep a service charge that represents work already done to issue the ticket (a ticket‑issuance/service fee), even if the airline cancelled the flight and a refund is due — provided that fee is not simply a payment/booking processing fee.  
- To be allowed, the fee must be:
  - clearly and prominently disclosed at the time of purchase (existence, amount, and if it is non‑refundable), and
  - charged on a per‑passenger basis.
- If the fee was not properly disclosed when the ticket was sold, the Department of Transportation would likely view retaining it as a deceptive practice and the fee should not be kept.
- The Department did not fully resolve whether a pure booking/payment processing fee can be retained when a refund is due, so that situation is less clear.

Other important points
- Refunds must be returned in the original form of payment unless you agree to a cash‑equivalent alternative.  
- If you believe a fee was kept but was not disclosed, you can challenge the agent and may report the matter to the DOT’s Aviation Consumer Protection office.
