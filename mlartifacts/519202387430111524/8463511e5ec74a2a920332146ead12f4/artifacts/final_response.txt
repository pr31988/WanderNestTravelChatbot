The information you provided from the DOT rule does not list any specific mandatory formatting or content requirements for travel vouchers themselves. It explains who may be eligible for travel credits/vouchers when travel is affected by a serious communicable disease and that airlines may require documentation (government quarantine/travel prohibition or medical documentation from a licensed treating professional) to verify eligibility. It also says carriers may rely on partners to issue vouchers and ticket agents should help supply needed info.

Because the KB does not specify mandatory voucher characteristics, you should confirm exact terms with the airline or the ticketing agent. To help you when you contact them, here are the common and important voucher details you should expect and confirm:

- Passenger name and contact information.  
- Original itinerary and ticket number(s).  
- Voucher number or reference code for easy tracking.  
- Monetary value of the credit (how it was calculated) or statement that it equals the fare paid.  
- Issue date and clear expiration date (and whether the airline will extend it in special circumstances).  
- Any restrictions (which fare classes, routes, or partner airlines it can be used on).  
- Whether the voucher is transferable or refundable.  
- Any fees or fare differences that will apply when rebooking.  
- Instructions for redemption and where to present required documentation.  
- Issuing carrier or entity name and contact details for customer service.

Also confirm the documentation the airline requires to obtain the voucher: per the DOT rule, airlines may require either government documentation showing you are prohibited or required to quarantine, or current medical documentation from a licensed treating medical professional advising you not to travel. Airlines are not required to accept only a passenger’s self-diagnosis unless a public-health directive says documentation shouldn’t be required.
