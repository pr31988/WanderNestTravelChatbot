Short answer: Sometimes — but only in limited circumstances and only if the fee was clearly and prominently disclosed when you bought the ticket.

What the Department of Transportation guidance says (key points)
- A ticket agent may retain a service charge that is for issuing the original ticket (a service independent of simply processing payment), even if the airline cancels or significantly changes the flight and you get a refund.  
- Any such retained fee must be assessed on a per-passenger basis, and its existence, amount, and non‑refundable nature (if applicable) must have been clearly and prominently disclosed to the consumer at the time of purchase.  
- If a fee was not disclosed at purchase, keeping it when issuing a refund is likely an unfair or deceptive practice and the DOT would consider action.  
- The DOT did not make a definitive decision in its final rule about whether a pure “booking/payment processing” fee can be kept; fees that are solely for processing payment (a booking fee that the consumer found) are treated differently and may not be retainable.  
- Refunds must be given in the original form of payment unless you agree to a different cash-equivalent form.

What you can do
- Check your booking receipt and the terms you were shown at purchase to see whether the fee and its non‑refundable status were clearly disclosed.  
- If the fee was not disclosed, ask the ticket agent for a refund of that fee and cite DOT rules; if they refuse, you can escalate to the DOT’s Office of Aviation Consumer Protection or dispute the charge with your card issuer.
