Short answer: Sometimes — but only if the fee meets strict conditions. If the ticket agent charged a service fee that was clearly and prominently disclosed at the time of purchase, is assessed on a per-passenger basis, and is for services beyond merely processing payment (for example, a fee for issuing the ticket), the agent may be allowed to retain that fee even when the airline cancels or significantly changes the flight and a refund is due. 

When a fee generally cannot be retained:
- If the fee was not clearly and prominently disclosed (including the amount and whether it is non‑refundable) at the time of purchase, retaining it is likely a deceptive practice and the agent should not keep it.  
- The Department has not definitively resolved whether a pure “booking fee” that is ONLY for processing payment (the fee for processing payment for a flight the consumer found) can be retained when the carrier cancels or changes the flight.

Other important points:
- Refunds must be provided in the original form of payment unless the consumer agrees to an alternative cash‑equivalent.
- The DOT will scrutinize fees that appear to circumvent the consumer’s right to a meaningful refund.

If this is your situation, recommended next steps:
1. Check your booking confirmation and the agent’s terms for any disclosure of the fee, its amount, and whether it is non‑refundable.  
2. Ask the ticket agent (and the airline, if different) to explain the fee and provide documentation showing it was disclosed at purchase.  
3. If you believe the fee was not properly disclosed or is being unfairly retained, escalate to the airline/ticket agent’s customer service and consider filing a complaint with the Department of Transportation’s Office of Aviation Consumer Protection.
