Helpful guidance you can use when an airline offers a voucher
- Ask the airline or ticket agent for the voucher’s full written terms before you accept it. Important items to confirm include:
  - monetary value or credit amount (and currency) and whether it equals the original fare/taxes or some other amount
  - issue date and expiration or last‑use date
  - whether the voucher is transferable or must be used only by the original passenger
  - which carriers/marketing partners and routes/classes the voucher can be used on
  - any blackout dates, change or rebooking fees, and how fare differences are handled
  - how to redeem the voucher and any booking deadlines or online/phone procedures
  - whether unused portions are refundable or convertible to cash under any circumstances
  - contact information for customer service and how disputes are handled

- Documentation the airline may require to issue a voucher
  - The DOT rule permits airlines to ask for documentation showing you are prohibited or required to quarantine by a government entity, or a current medical note from a licensed treating medical professional advising you not to travel (including if you have or may have a serious communicable disease). An airline may require such documentation unless HHS issues a directive saying requiring documentation is not in the public interest.
  - For ticket agent‑purchased itineraries, airlines may rely on partners or ticket agents to help process and issue the voucher; ticket agents are encouraged to provide contact info and purchase price to the carrier if needed.

If you want a definitive list of mandatory voucher characteristics, please contact the airline or the ticket agent who issued your booking — they will provide the specific voucher terms they use in these situations.
