Yes. Under DOT guidance, a practice is "deceptive" if it is likely to mislead a consumer, acting reasonably under the circumstances, about a material matter. A "material" matter is one that would likely affect the consumer’s conduct or decision (for example, whether a fee is charged or whether a refund is available). The DOT treats misleading information about privacy practices, undisclosed fees, or failure to disclose that a fee is nonrefundable as deceptive. The Department may investigate and impose penalties for such deceptive practices.

If you believe an airline or ticket agent misled you, keep copies of communications and receipts and you can submit a complaint to the DOT’s aviation consumer protection office for review.
