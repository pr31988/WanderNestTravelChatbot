Yes. Under the DOT’s guidance a practice is “deceptive” if it is likely to mislead a consumer, acting reasonably under the circumstances, about a material matter. A “material” matter is one that would likely have affected the consumer’s decision or conduct. 

Examples from DOT guidance include:
- failing to disclose fees (or that a fee is non‑refundable), which can mislead a consumer to believe the full amount paid is refundable; and
- failing to disclose the existence or amount of a refund fee.

In the privacy context, the DOT also treats violations of a carrier’s privacy policy, COPPA/FTC rules, or Data Privacy Framework principles as potentially unfair or deceptive. The Department’s Office of Aviation Consumer Protection can investigate complaints and impose penalties where appropriate.

If you think an airline or ticket agent misled you about a material issue (fees, refunds, privacy practices, etc.), you can document the communications and consider filing a complaint with the DOT — I can help summarize what to include if you’d like.
